# FS-01 — Timing and arrangement

**Status:** Draft. **Dependencies:** `foundation`, `timeline`, project command model. **PRD:** §§9–11, 13.

## User-visible contract

- **TA-01** A project has one global tempo map. The default clip/track-automation timebase is musical; users can pin eligible clips and automation to elapsed time. The current mode is visible in the arrangement and persisted.
- **TA-02** Tempo events may be instantaneous, linear BPM ramps, or defined ease-in/ease-out ramps. Global snap applies when editing unless disabled. Tempo remains positive and segment order is valid; invalid edits produce an error and leave the old map unchanged.
- The tempo lane under the ruler draws the tempo as it plays and edits it (ADR-0112). Each of these is one undo step:
  - a drawn ramp keeps the tempo at its start, removes the points inside it, and holds its end tempo until the next point;
  - a point can be moved, added or removed;
  - each ramp has a shape: straight, S-curve, ease in, ease out or hold;
  - "Ramp to this track's tempo" ramps across an audio clip's transition to its reviewed BPM.

  The last point always holds.
- **TA-03** Bars/beats, elapsed time, and sample locations refer to one authoritative conversion implementation at each supported sample rate. The UI, playback, MIDI, warp, automation, and export use the same tempo-map revision. The arrangement has a clickable musical ruler with a moving vertical playhead line and a second elapsed-time ruler below the pinned master row. Elapsed labels follow the tempo map, including ramps and changes, rather than assuming a fixed BPM.
- **TA-04** Changing an audio or MIDI clip's timebase preserves its current audible sample boundaries and changes how later tempo edits anchor it. MIDI note positions remain clip-local and retain their current scheduled sample positions at conversion. The current mode and the action to switch it are visible in the arrangement. The transformation is one undo step; a boundary that cannot be represented exactly in the target timebase rejects without moving the clip. Changing an automation lane's timebase preserves each breakpoint's current absolute sample position. Clip-owned breakpoints remain relative to their clip's start. The conversion is one undoable edit, retains lane and point IDs, and rejects atomically if an exact target coordinate cannot be represented or points would collide. Interpolation between breakpoints follows the selected timebase, so tempo ramps may change the values between otherwise fixed breakpoints; later tempo edits move musical anchors and leave elapsed anchors at their sample positions.
- **TA-05** Arrangement navigation includes explicit snap state, zoom, markers, loop region and locators. A click on the bar ruler seeks; dragging across its bars selects and enables a whole-bar loop, shown in the ruler. The LOOP control toggles the selected range without discarding it. Clip operations include split, move, duplicate, delete, trim, slip, stretch and per-clip fades. Extending a trim reveals source material where present.
- **TA-05a** Audio clip fades are instance settings. Each edge has a duration as a fraction of the clip's rendered interval, from zero to one; the two fractions sum to at most one. Trimming or tempo edits therefore scale the effective fade with that clip without changing its stored shape. Linear and smoothstep curves are selectable independently for each edge. A fade begins or ends at silence exactly at the corresponding clip boundary, and playback, real-time export and offline export use the same sample-indexed gain evaluator. Changing a fade is one undoable edit and does not modify source media. A one-frame clip cannot carry a nonzero fade.
- **TA-05b** Duplicate-after places one copy at the original clip's end in its own timebase, preserving source identity, source range, fades and clip automation settings. A copied clip receives fresh clip, automation-lane and automation-point IDs so later edits address only that instance. It fails without an edit if a neighbouring clip occupies that interval or identity allocation is exhausted. Deleting a clip removes only that instance; source media remains available in the project. Each action is one undo step, with the result surviving save and reopen.
- **TA-05c** Dragging a MIDI clip moves it to a bar boundary in the arrangement. A musical clip retains its tick duration; an elapsed clip retains its exact sample duration and moves its sample anchor to the selected bar under the current tempo map. The preview snaps by bar. A collision or invalid destination rejects the complete edit. One completed drag is one undo step, and moving to the same position creates none.
- **TA-05c** Splitting an audio clip at an interior timeline boundary creates two adjacent instances of the same source with contiguous source ranges. The visible placement and audible fade envelope remain unchanged across the operation; no fade is added at the cut. A split is one undoable edit and source media is never modified. Rate-converted source edges use the same frame mapping as playback and export. A split that cannot preserve that mapping or the clip envelope must reject the edit explicitly.
- **TA-05d** Slip editing shifts the clip instance's source window inside the available source media while its timeline placement, length, fades and attached settings remain fixed. The interface provides a Shift+Alt-drag gesture (Shift- and Ctrl-click add clips to the selection) with bounded source movement and an explicit rejection if a requested range exceeds available media or changes the rendered duration after source-rate conversion. A completed slip is one undo step and survives save/reopen.
- **TA-05e** Trimming a rate-converted audio clip uses the same source-frame to project-sample mapping as playback and export. Its visible boundary and source window must describe the same duration without an implicit stretch. If the requested project sample falls between representable source-frame boundaries, the edit rejects atomically and explains the limit. Revealing a prior trim restores available original media.
- **TA-06** Same-track clip active intervals are disjoint in rendered project-sample coordinates. Move, duplicate and resize commands either constrain to a valid interval with visible feedback or reject atomically. Tempo, meter, timebase and project-rate changes revalidate all affected clips, including mixed musical/elapsed anchors; any new overlap or zero-length interval rejects the whole edit with conflicting clip IDs.
- **TA-07** Multi-track edits are one atomic command. One gesture is one undo step. Undo and redo restore clip geometry, source offset, timebase and associated automation together.
- **TA-08** MIDI clips use the same musical positions and tempo map. Duplicate-after places a copy at the current clip's end in its own timebase, preserving pattern length, loop settings, clip-local note timing, velocities and automation settings while assigning fresh clip, note, lane and point IDs. It rejects an occupied destination atomically, is one undo step, and survives save/reopen. Delete removes only the selected MIDI clip and its attached notes and automation; Undo restores the whole clip in one step. Drawn notes retain intended timing and duration after duplication and looping. No v1 recording path is implied.
- **TA-09** A project has an editable time-signature map. Meter changes occur only at bar boundaries, define continuous bar numbering and beat grouping, and survive save/reopen. Bars/beats ruler, bar and beat snap, MIDI looping and export use the same meter-map revision; a tempo change does not move a meter event's musical position.
- **TA-10** Changing the project nominal sample rate is one undoable command performed while transport is stopped. Musical anchors retain their beat positions. Elapsed-time-pinned clips and automation retain their elapsed seconds: their integer sample coordinates convert by the exact old/new rate ratio, rounded to nearest sample with ties to even; overflow or invalid rates reject atomically. The command updates the manifest, invalidates rate-dependent processing/prefetch generations and requires device renegotiation before playback. Undo restores the old rate, exact prior coordinates and a new cache generation.

## Component contract

`TickPosition` and `SamplePosition` are distinct signed 64-bit types; raw integers cannot be mixed in public APIs. The fixed scale is 960,000,000 ticks per quarter note, and the manifest records its version. Tempo and meter maps are immutable after construction and each carries a revision ID. Tempo events require positive finite BPM, strictly increasing ticks and an initial event at zero. `Step` holds BPM; `LinearBpm` is linear in quarter-note beats. Ease-in, ease-out and ease-in-out use fixed piecewise-linear BPM knots; exact equations and knot positions are in [ADR-0007](../decisions/0007-timing-representation.md). Segment boundaries are half-open and belong to the new event. Forward conversion accumulates elapsed time continuously and rounds once to the nearest sample, ties to even; inverse conversion is monotone and rounds to the nearest tick. Invalid rates, positions, meter events or overflow return an error.

Meter numerators are 1–32 and denominators are powers of two from 1 to 64. Events are unique, require one at tick zero, and may change only at a whole bar boundary under the preceding meter. Bar 1, beat 1 starts at tick zero; numbering is continuous and v1 has no pickup bar. Serialization stores exact event positions and parameters, not only sampled points. Looping does not reset the authoritative bar numbering; the UI may show loop-relative position separately.

Clip coordinates have an explicit anchor type. A clip instance references a source ID plus source range; moving or trimming never mutates source media. The arrangement model checks overlap on every command, including undo/redo and migration. Fade duration rounds to the nearest rendered frame, ties to even, when preparing a clip; the sample at the first fade-in boundary and last fade-out boundary is zero, with the gain reaching one just outside each fade. Smoothstep uses `u²(3−2u)` on the clamped linear progress `u`. Warp markers are ordered, anchored and cannot invert source time. The first timing-contract PR fixes tick scale, ramp equations, boundary rounding and error tolerances in this spec before dependent agents start.
