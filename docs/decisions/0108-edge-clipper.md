# ADR-0108 — Edge: a clipper under a true-peak ceiling

**Status:** Accepted and implemented; superseded in part by [ADR-0113](0113-edge-adjustable-ceiling.md), which makes the 0 dBFS ceiling adjustable. **Date:** 8 November 2026.
**Builds on:** ADR-0040 (native effect platform), ADR-0043 (one native insert path), ADR-0047 (Ceiling), ADR-0062 (denormals flushed).
**Contract:** `include/oscillate/native_dsp/effects/edge.hpp`, the table `src/native_dsp/effects/edge_table.hpp`, the DSP `src/native_dsp/effects/edge_dsp.hpp`.

## Context

Drums and masters are made louder by clipping their peaks rather than limiting them: a clipper changes the waveform for a few samples instead of turning the whole signal down for milliseconds, so kicks keep their punch. Forge's Clip curve has no ceiling, shape control, delta or metering, and Ceiling is a limiter. A clipper needs three things a saturator does not: an output that never passes its ceiling, including between samples; clipping that does not alias, since a flat top is full of harmonics; and an honest way to hear what it does.

## Decision

1. **A native effect, Edge (id `edge`),** on the effect platform: a parameter table and a kernel, no document, no sidechain. Parameters: Input (−12 to +24 dB), Shape (0 to 1), Output (−24 to 0 dB), Oversampling (1×, 2×, 4×, 8×, 16×), Filters (linear phase or light), Stereo Link, Delta, Compare and Level Match.
2. **The curve.** One continuous control from hard to soft: the knee is `0.6 · shape` wide; below `1 − knee` the curve is the input exactly, above it `threshold + knee · tanh((|x| − threshold) / knee)`, which approaches the 0 dBFS ceiling only at infinity. Shape 0 is a clamp at ±1.
3. **Oversampling.** A cascade of Kaiser-windowed linear-phase half-band FIRs, one per doubling. The first stage keeps 0.45 of the rate flat (21.6 kHz at 48 kHz) and attenuates 120 dB from 0.55; later stages only keep that band clear of their images. Light mode keeps 0.40 at 90 dB, with about a third of the taps and latency. From 8×, the curve runs with first-order antiderivative antialiasing (its mean over each top-rate step), and the top stage decimates through an even-length low-pass whose half-sample delay squares the antialiasing's, so the round trip stays whole samples; below 8× the half-sample mean would dull the top octave. A pad at the top rate makes the round trip a whole number of frames. Measured: a 10 kHz sine 12 dB over, hard-clipped at 16×, aliases under −100 dBFS from 20 Hz to 20 kHz.
4. **Stereo link** gives both channels the gain the louder one needs (with antialiasing, the louder one's antialiased curve over its mean), so the image does not move; unlinked, each channel clips alone. Switching fades over 20 ms.
5. **The true-peak guarantee.** Filtering a clipped signal brings back overshoot (Gibbs), so the clipper alone cannot promise 0 dBTP. After the filters a guard reads every interval between two frames with the BS.1770 4× interpolator the true-peak meter uses (`dsp::bs1770::TruePeak`), holds the gain each interval needs over every frame its interpolation reads, averages it over 0.5 ms and releases over 50 ms; the gain under each interval's interpolation is then flat and under what it needs. Its target is 0.01 dB under the ceiling, for the float rounding of the output. Output gain only attenuates. Measured with `StereoTruePeakMeter`: at or under 0 dBTP at every factor, filter, shape and link, on noise, kicks and a 10 kHz sine 12 dB over.
6. **Latency** is the round trip plus the guard (its 0.5 ms average and twice the interpolator's radius less one): 55 frames at 1×, 148 at 16× (linear phase) at 48 kHz. Every factor and filter choice has its own latency, so any change of them takes the route's rebuild path; they are not automatable.
7. **Delta and compare.** Delta plays the driven input, delayed by the latency, less the output, both after Output gain: output plus delta is the input times both gains (measured to 1e-5). Compare plays the input delayed by the latency, matched to the output's power over about 1.5 s (within ±24 dB) when Level Match is on. Output, delta and compare cross-fade over 20 ms.
8. **Telemetry:** the driven input's peak and the output's true peak per channel (held 1 s), how far the loudest peak is clipped (the curve's reduction at the top rate plus the guard's, held 1 s), the transfer dot (the input's recent peak and the curve's output for it), the latency in milliseconds, the factor, and the most clipping in each of the last 16 beats (half a second each while stopped).
9. **Presets,** measured on the source each is for, peaking at −1 dBFS: Kick Punch (+3 dB, nearly hard; clips 2.9 dB on a kick), Drum Bus (+2 dB, shape 0.35; 3.2 dB on a kit), 808 (+4 dB, soft, 4×; 3.1 dB on an 808) and Master (+2 dB, shape 0.5, 16×; 1.5 dB on a mix).
10. **Panel** (`qml/edge`): the transfer curve from the engine (`edge_transfer`) with the dashed ceiling and the signal's dot, the per-beat clip history under it; Input, Shape and Output; oversampling as segments with the latency and the light filters; Stereo link, Delta and Level-matched compare; the input peak, the clipped dB and the output true peak; the level-matched A/B in the header.

## Consequences

- **Tests:** `native_edge_contract` (shape 0 is a clamp, no aliasing at 16×, the true-peak ceiling, delta nulls, the latency, stereo link, the matched compare, block splits, export against live, hostile input, restrained presets, no allocation, telemetry) and `quick_edge_panel_contract` (every parameter has a control, the text fits, controls, the curve, telemetry, the real Edge, presets; screenshots with `EDGE_PANEL_SCREENSHOT_DIR`). `effect_export_parity` and the platform contracts cover it with every engine.
- **CPU:** at 16× linear phase, about 600 multiply-adds per channel per frame in the filters, the curve 16 times, and three 33-tap interpolations per channel in the guard and the output meter.
- **The guard is a limiter.** Clipping noise-like material hard leaves several dB of overshoot after the filters, which the guard takes off with its 50 ms release; a hot drum bus clips less and is limited a little more than a clipper alone would.
