Kick & Bass Lock

Below about 120 Hz, a kick's tail and a bassline are two sine waves fighting over the same space. When their phases disagree they cancel, and the low end goes thin, wobbles from bar to bar and falls apart in mono. Lock fixes it where the sound is made, because both instruments are ours: Anvil tunes its tail to the bass, the later sound starts in phase with the one already playing, Nova's sub ducks under the kick's exact shape (no detector, no latency), and the lows go mono. The scope shows one beat below 120 Hz; red is energy lost to cancellation. Press Play, then flip Lock.

How Lock works
  1. Tune. Anvil's tail moves to the bass note (or the key) at each hit, so kick and bass share a pitch and can add instead of beat.
  2. Align. Whichever starts second starts in phase with the one already sounding: a bass note during a kick takes the kick's phase, and a kick over a held note picks its start phase so its tail lands on the bass. Both synths know each other's notes ahead from the project, so it is exact.
  3. Duck. Nova's sub dips under the kick's exact volume curve, below the crossover only. No detector, no lookahead latency, no attack or release to set.
  4. Mono. Below the crossover, Nova's unison and stereo spread fold to mono, so the club system sums what you heard.
In the DAW: Lock kick & bass appears in the track menu of any Nova track when the project has an Anvil track, and as a section on both panels. It is one undo step and stays live as you edit either part.
