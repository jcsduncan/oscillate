Knock

Eight pads of physically modelled percussion. Each hit is something striking (a mallet, stick, finger or brush) something that rings (wood, skin, metal, glass, tube or card), optionally a second resonator it sets ringing, and a body around it. Tone runs from a knock on a thing to a pitched note. Finish makes it modern: Snap, a hard Length gate, Lift to clear the low end, and Crush. Pick a kit for a genre. Width spreads each pad's ringing modes and moves its hits. Pick a loop from the library, or Generate one: Roll, Mutate, lock the lanes you like, and let it Evolve. Press Play, or click a pad to hear it. Every sound here is synthesised in your browser from these controls.

How a hit is made

  • Striker. Hardness sets how long it stays in contact with the surface. A short contact excites high modes, which makes it brighter. Velocity makes the hit harder, as with a real mallet. Click is the contact noise.
  • Resonator. Each material has its own mode pattern: a wooden bar's modes are wide apart, a skin's are dense and close, glass rings for a long time. Where you strike sets which modes sound.
  • Coupled. A second resonator rung by the first, such as a skin on a box, a plate on a tube, or two glasses beating against each other.
  • Body. The box around it: its low modes and hollow air.

Built from what we have

  • Modal library: the felt contact law, the modal resonators and a body resonator live in a shared library (modal_dsp) that Knock strikes.
  • Pulse and Anvil: the falling pitch of a slack skin (Slack) and drawn pitch curves.
  • Ember: seeded variation (Hand), so repeated hits differ but every export matches playback.
  • Crate: later, a striker made from your own recording.

Loops and effects

  • The pads take MIDI notes C1 to G1. Velocity changes the tone, not only the level.
  • Each pad has its own pan and room send. Knock renders one stereo output.
  • Patterns come from MIDI clips and note effects (chance, ratchets, echo), so there is no second sequencer to learn.
