Nova Analogue

Real analogue synths sound alive because no two voices are built alike: each voice card is a little sharp or flat, brighter or darker, slower to open, and everything drifts as it warms up. Analogue gives every Nova voice (every note you play, and every unison voice) its own fixed offsets from a seed, plus slow drift. Age turns it all up at once; Voice Lanes shows what each voice got and lets you paint any of it by hand.

How it works
Fixed per voice, repeatableEach voice's offsets come from the seed and its number, so voice 3 is always "the slightly sharp, dark one" and an export sounds exactly like playback. New synth rolls a new set.
Age scales, lanes refineTolerances set how far parts can differ; Age scales them together. Painting a lane overrides that voice only and survives Age changes (amber). Back to seed undoes it.
Drift moves, tolerances stayDrift is a slow, separate wander per voice on pitch and cutoff (Rate, Amount). Free-running oscillators keep running between notes, so chords never start in phase.
