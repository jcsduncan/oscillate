Why a pan LFO isn't enough

A pan control only changes how loud the sound is in each speaker. A real pass also changes pitch, level, brightness, the timing between your ears and how much of the room you hear, all at once and in step. Flyby works all of them out from one moving position.

How it works

  • Doppler: each ear hears the source through its own delay line, set to that ear's distance divided by the speed of sound, 343 m/s. As the distance changes, the delay changes, and that moves the pitch.
  • Ear timing: the same per-ear delays put up to about 0.65 ms between your ears. The ear in your head's shadow also loses its highs (a spherical head model).
  • Distance: the level falls with 1/r, and air absorption darkens the sound as it moves away.
  • Room: as the source moves away you hear more of the reflections and less of the source itself.

In the DAW

  • A native effect for any track or bus, and a module in Nova's FX rack.
  • Run: Loop, Once per MIDI note, Sync to bars, or Position, which you automate to place the pass exactly on the timeline.
  • On time at the closest point: the travel delay is measured from the path's closest point, so the moment it passes you lands on the beat and the effect adds no latency.
  • Headphones use the ear timing and head shadow. Speakers use a constant-power pan, so no ear timing reaches both ears.
